事件

FBIによると、2025年に米国人は届かなかった注文で5億300万ドルを失いました。私たちは、存在しない荷物について15回も追跡通知を受け取ったウェールズの71歳女性を追い、偽ショップと配達SMSの裏にある「工場」を明らかにし、逮捕と懲役8年半の判決、そして偽サイトが唯一偽れないものを紹介します。

覚えておきたいこと

  1. FBI：2025年、届かなかった購入品による被害は5億300万ドル（さらにクレジットカード詐欺で2億8,200万ドル）。
  2. 偽ショップの多くは開設から90日未満で、約85%が2週間以内に消えます。
  3. 売り手がギフトカードを求めたら、それは詐欺です。必ず。

よくある質問

サイトが本物か見分けるには？

店のアドレスを自分で入力し（広告からではなく）、アドレスを「scam」という語と一緒に検索し、サイトの作成日を確認してください（無料の公式検索：lookup.icann.org — amazon.comは「Created: 1994」と表示）。開設数週間のサイトで有名ブランドが90%オフなら危険信号です。

鍵マーク（https）があれば十分？

いいえ。FBIは通販サイトにはhttpsが必要としていますが、偽ショップにも鍵マークはあります。

最も安全な支払い方法は？

クレジットカードです。請求に異議を申し立てられます。FBIは、売り手への送金やギフトカードでの支払いは絶対にしないよう勧めています。

偽サイトで買ってしまった——どうすれば？

まず銀行かカード会社に電話し、次に通報してください：米国 — ic3.gov と ReportFraud.ftc.gov · 英国 — Report Fraud、0300 123 2040 · カナダ — Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre、1-888-495-8501 · オーストラリア — scamwatch.gov.au。日本では警察相談専用電話 #9110 へ。

注文していない荷物のSMSが届くのはなぜ？

多くは偽ショップと同じフィッシング「工場」から送られています。リンクはタップしないでください。英国では詐欺SMSを7726に転送できます。

0:00Black Friday deals 2026: 10,000 fake stores

Black Friday deals, 2026. Before you pay, stop. 10,000 fake stores copy your brands, except one thing. But why do you never check?

The FBI says Americans lost five hundred three million dollars last year to orders that never came. A 71-year-old in Wales got 15 tracking updates for a parcel that never existed. And that text about your Black Friday package? It may come from the same kind of factory London police already shut down.

Everyone says: look for the padlock. Fake stores have the padlock too. By the end, you'll know the one thing they can't fake, and how to pay so you can dispute the charge.

0:35The perfect store: a fake website that looked real

October 2025. Swansea, Wales. David Bevan, 71, a retired university data officer, is scrolling Facebook Marketplace. He finds a garden storage unit from a brand he knows, Keter, at about half the price he's seen anywhere else. £80.

He'd been on the real Keter website before, so the layout and the photos looked familiar. The site was called Keter Outlet. He paid by credit card. The next day, an order confirmation arrived, with an international tracking number.

Everything looked right. Then the updates started. Shipped. Customs.

Handed over to a local delivery company. Then: missed delivery, eight twenty-seven in the morning. David was at home. Another attempt was promised.

Nothing came. About 15 tracking updates, for a parcel that, as far as anyone can tell, never existed. His emails went unanswered. It's the same story in America.

In Detroit, an FBI special agent put it simply: these sites look like the real company, but they're not. One shopper told the Better Business Bureau she paid $74.90 for a sweater from a Facebook ad. It never came. The scammers have created a website that looks just like the actual retail site.

Sometimes you're buying something that you think is some amazing Black Friday deal, but it's too good to be true. Remember this number. In 2025, Americans reported losing five hundred three million dollars to purchases that never arrived, and another two hundred eighty-two million to credit card fraud. That's only what people reported to the FBI.

And David's store wasn't a one-off. It was one page from a catalog. Look at how this works. Scammers need you in a hurry.

90% off. Only two left. A timer says the sale ends in half an hour. It's all designed for that.

Your job here is simple: a cold, calm look. Close the tab. Come back the next day. A real store will still be there.

The discounts will still be there. A fake phishing store will be gone.

2:3910,000 fake stores, one template (even AI recommended them)

This month, researchers at NordVPN mapped more than 10,000 fake stores, all built from one template, copying a hundred sixty-six fashion and luxury brands in 26 countries. On November 3, one thousand five hundred forty-nine of them were fully live, taking orders. Adidas alone had 52 fake domains. Ralph Lauren, 30.

These stores don't even need paid ads. They show up in search results. At checkout, they show PayPal and Klarna logos. According to the researchers, none of them actually accept either.

And here's the part that should worry you: in their tests, an AI assistant, Perplexity, recommended two of these fake stores as trustworthy. One scam-tracking app says its users report a new fake store about every three minutes. Around 500 a day. Holiday-themed fakes jumped fivefold in two months.

Most of these stores are less than 90 days old. And about 85% disappear within two weeks. By the time you complain, the store is gone. Then investigators in Austria opened the code of one fake shop.

Inside, they found a list of more than two thousand five hundred other web addresses. Future stores, already prepared. Fewer than a third were even switched on yet. They aren't building one store at a time.

They're building next week's. In America, the Better Business Bureau says online purchase scams made up 28% of all scams reported to it last year, and about nine in ten people who reported one lost money. The most copied names: PayPal, Amazon, Target. In Australia, regulators warn about "ghost stores": fake local shops with a sad story and a closing-down sale.

But a fake store is only half the machine. The other half is already in your pocket. If you're buying something online, they are getting not just your money, but they're getting your name, your credit card information, they're getting your address,

4:24The factory behind the "package delivery" text + how to tell if a website is legit

That's the real product. Not the £80 parcel. Your card, your name, your address. And the people who steal them don't build websites themselves.

They rent them, from factories. In London, police took apart one of them. It was called LabHost. It offered ready-made copies of a hundred eighty-five websites, banks, government services, and companies including Amazon.

More than two thousand criminals used it. When police seized the logs, they counted four hundred twenty-nine thousand, one hundred fourteen stolen card numbers, and nearly nine hundred thousand passwords. Across the Atlantic, Google went to federal court in New York against another factory, called Darcula. Google alleges it powered fake texts pretending to be the U.S.

Postal Service and the IRS, and stole almost nine hundred thousand credit cards, nearly forty thousand of them from Americans. It's the same kind of assembly line behind the spam texts we investigated last time. The text about your parcel and the fake store are two doors into one building. So here's the one thing they can't fake.

They can copy the logo, the photos, the reviews, even the padlock. But they can't fake the day the website was born. A famous brand doesn't open a new website three weeks before Black Friday and sell at 90% off. Most fake stores are less than 90 days old.

That's your tell. Before I pay, I always take a few simple steps. I copy the store's web address and add the word "scam," to see whether this store has lived up to people's expectations, or not. The second thing you can do: just look up the website's creation date.

If the site was created two weeks ago and sells brands at half price, I close it right away. Subscribe to our channel. Every week we publish new investigations for your safety.

6:13Police strike back: LabHost shut down, 8½ years in prison

April 2024. The Metropolitan Police and partners pulled the plug on LabHost. Officers searched more than 70 addresses across the U.K. and arrested 24 people. Then they did something unusual: they contacted around twenty-five thousand British victims directly, to tell them their details had been stolen.

Its co-creator, Zak Coyne from Huddersfield, pleaded guilty. On April 14, 2025, Manchester Crown Court sentenced him to 8½ years in prison. The factory that copied Amazon is closed. But its customers didn't all retire.

In America, the fight is moving to court. Google is suing the text factories. And in July, the Justice Department charged a 38-year-old man from Massachusetts, accusing him of running a warehouse in New Hampshire where gift cards stolen from scam victims were turned into electronics and shipped to China. He is presumed innocent.

The case was investigated by Homeland Security Investigations, the IRS, and the Postal Inspection Service. Gift cards are the scammers' favorite money. Homeland Security even runs a nationwide operation against it, called Project Red Hook. And it can start before the card is ever given.

If you purchase a gift card in the store, it has no value on it, right? But scammers will steal the information off the back of the cards, sometimes paying people that they have employed to go to a store, steal the information off the cards, and then when the card is purchased by a, you know, you or I to give a gift to someone via the gift card, that gift card balance now is immediately drained by the scammer. So before you buy one as a present, the FBI says, check the packaging and the code strip for any sign of tampering. And remember this: if a seller asks you to pay with a gift card, it's a scam.

Every time. But the most dangerous moment of Black Friday isn't when you pay. It's the phone call a few days later. When your banker calls you and says, you've been making a lot of strange purchases, you think, maybe I have, I'm buying from companies that I wouldn't normally buy from for giving gifts, and it seems like it could be a believable phone call, but it's usually not.

8:12The second call: the fake "bank" call after Black Friday

That's the trap. After a week of shopping, a call about strange purchases sounds believable. Your bank won't ask for your password or a code. Hang up, and call the number on the back of your card yourself.

So, four things before you pay this Black Friday. One: use a credit card, so you can dispute the charge. David paid by credit card, and his bank is his best chance. Two: never pay a seller by wire, gift card, or crypto.

Three: don't buy from an ad. Type the store's address yourself. Four: check the day the site was born. If it already happened, call your bank first.

Then report it. In the U.S., to the FBI at ic3.gov, and to the FTC. In the U.K., to Report Fraud, and forward scam texts to 7726 In Canada, the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre. In Australia, Scamwatch.

Every report helps them find the factory. The people who lose the most to this are our loved ones. They're polite, and they trust good-looking websites. Send them this video today, and agree on one rule: before they buy anything from a slick ad, they send you the link, so you can double-check everything.

If this video protects your parents, hit like, and subscribe: new investigations every week. The FBI in San Francisco put it in seven words: pause before you pay, confirm before you click.
